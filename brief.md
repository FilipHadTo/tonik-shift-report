### What's the task?

You'll design & build the **Create Shift Report** experience for a web product used by closing managers at bars and nightclubs.

The people using this tool are wrapping up a 10-hour shift at 3am, tired, and ready to go home. However, they need to report the shift’s events to brief the next team and keep a record of important occurrences, especially incidents that could impact their insurance. Your job is to design something that allows them to work fast, is forgiving, and is smart enough to do some of the heavy lifting for them. This is an **AI-assisted product,** so think less "form to fill out", more "AI-assisted flow that meets them where they're at."

When a closing manager opens this product at 3am, we want them to think: _"This is actually faster than what I was doing before. And I didn't forget anything."_

Two tensions your prototype has to resolve:

- **Guided vs. freeform.** Some managers want to brain-dump in natural language, others want structure to lean on. Support both without forcing a choice upfront.
- **Friction kills it.** The people with the most useful context have the least motivation to capture it at 3am. Target: under 10 minutes for a typical night.

#### Broader product context

This product is being built for a company in the nightlife risk and compliance space. The shift report is their core feature: it's how venues build an evidence trail that protects them legally and operationally.

**Audience**

- Closing managers at bars and nightclubs – the primary users of this flow. Experienced, time-pressured, finishing their night at a laptop before they can leave.
- Venue owners / operators consume the reports, but they're not your user for this task.

**User goal:** A closing manager should be able to capture a complete, legally defensible shift report in under 10 minutes, without feeling like they're doing paperwork.

**Success metrics (early indicators)**

- Manager finishes their end-of-night faster than with their current process (group texts, spreadsheets, etc.)
- Incident documentation is captured close to the event, with evidence attached
- Reports are complete enough to hold up in a legal or insurance context, without requiring perfection from the manager

**What the full report covers**

In the real product, a report can include: general overview · incidents · audience overview, experience, safety & concerns · security overview · artist and crew experience · door staff review · production team / run of show / doors review · bar staff review · kitchen · other third-party vendors · private events · maintenance / equipment issues · inventory notes.

All sections are optional from the manager's perspective – the system should accept whatever they have and still produce a useful report. Your flow sketch should account for this; your prototype only needs the incident path plus a visible stub of the rest.

**Incidents are the special case**, and the one that matters most: each one needs at minimum a time, location, type, and description. Managers can attach photos, videos, and documents to the report or to specific sections (stubbed is fine).

Where this brief is silent, make a call. We want to see your judgment, not just your execution.

### Deliverables

#### **1. Lock your scope for the end-of-shift prototype.**

Consider the users, the touchpoints, the environment, and think about the whole closing flow. Remember that the incidents are important to include in the report.

- The manager needs to create the report at the end of the night
- User enters raw captures of what happened (text, photos, whatever they have)
- AI detected important information → surfaces it for review
- Manager corrects or confirms → it's attached to the report.

Create whatever works for you — a flow, a note, a video of your train of thought, Markdown files, paper sketches — that will explain your scope. Leave it deliberately unpolished; ~1 hour.

#### **2. Build a proof of concept in code**

Put your scope to the test and build the prototype in code. Propose how this tool will look, how users will actually interact with it, and how the AI will assist the user in reaching their goal. We’re not looking for pixel-perfect, but we want to understand the creative direction you want this tool to take. ~6 hours.

- Create a working prototype and a deployed or shareable link.
- Use whatever tools you like – part of what we're evaluating is _how_ you use them, not which ones. Free tiers are fine; if a paid tool is your workflow and it's a blocker, tell Dorota.
- Send link or access to your repo, so we can see how it's built.

#### **3. A Loom/Supercut (max 15 min, unedited, casual).**

- Walk us through your process and decision-making path
- Cover how your flow works and how it solves the outlined problems
- Explain the creative direction of this prototype
- Tell us what you would do next if you had more time
